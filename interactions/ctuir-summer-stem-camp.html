CTUIR Summer STEM Camp (Summer 25)

I co-facilitated a week-long curriculum on sound and listening for elementary students at Washington Elementary School in Pendleton, Oregon, as part of the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation (CTUIR) summer STEM camp. The activities are designed by my colleagues.

Monday, Sound Detectives
Students introduced themselves with a signature sound, then took turns blindfolded, identifying objects hidden in a bag by sound alone and describing what they heard in words.

Tuesday, Sound Collectors
Students went on a scavenger hunt indoors and outdoors, recording environmental sounds on phones, titling each recording, then drawing the sounds they captured.

Wednesday, Bending and Blending Sounds
Students listened to their recordings from Tuesday played at different speeds and in reverse, described how each version felt different, and edited sounds themselves with browser-based audio tools.

Thursday, Sound and Numbers
Students rotated through stations built around sound variables like pitch and tempo, solving problems by listening, then created sonic graphs to convey differences in height data between zoo animals, aliens, and humans using Chrome Music Lab or their voices.

Friday, Elastic Band Guitars
Students built and decorated guitars from different materials like tissue boxes and elastic bands of different thicknesses, exploring how vibration produces sound, and closed the week with a mini performance.
