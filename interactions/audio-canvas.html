Audio Canvas (spring 26)

Audio Canvas is an exploratory concept for translating drawings into sound. Recent text-to-sound models can generate audio for virtually any input. The system cannot refuse or say there is no corresponding sound. This makes it possible to sonify things that have no obvious sonic identity, raising questions about sound representability that were previously unaskable.

What makes something "sonically friendly" or "sonically elusive?" "Rain" carries a relatively clear sonic signature. What about "flowers?" Probably less so. Audio Canvas treats this distinction as a site of inquiry, using generative sound as a probe to study the "sonically friendly / elusive things."

The process sends a drawing through two generative stages. A visual-language model writes text prompts from the image. A text-to-sound model converts those prompts into audio. Varying the prompt instructions reveals how wording and visual context steer what the system attends to.

Across experiments, the system gravitated toward sonically obvious elements and overlooked the rest. Cropping smaller regions showed that the same pixels produce different sonic descriptions depending on surrounding context. Sonically elusive elements produced sounds that were often harder to describe and impossible to judge as "accurate." The lost-in-translation gap surfaced at every stage.

Audio Canvas explores what it means to sonify everything, including the things that resist being heard.
