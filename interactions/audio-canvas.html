Audio Canvas (Spring 26)

Audio Canvas is an exploratory concept for translating sketches into sound. Recent text-to-sound models can generate audio for virtually any text input. The system cannot refuse or say there is no corresponding sound. This opens up the possibility of translating things that have no obvious sonic identity into sound.

What makes something "sonically friendly" or "sonically elusive?" "Rain" carries a relatively identifiable sonic signature. What about something like "lotus," "solitude," or "human mind?" Probably less so. My interest is to treat this distinction as a starting point, using generative sound to probe (1) how generative AI systems, as cultural technologies, reveal our shared sonic associations, (2) a more playful, speculative way to sketch.

The playful explorations: I would first quickly sketch something, thinking about how different elements in my sketch might sound like as I sketch them. A vision-language model then interprets the sketches and writes a set of text prompts based on different instructions. I would also come up with some prompts based on my own interpretation. Lastly, a text-to-sound model converts those prompts into audio for me to listen and react to.

Across experiments, the system gravitated toward sonically obvious elements and overlooked the rest. Cropping smaller regions showed that the same pixels produce different sonic descriptions depending on surrounding context. Sonically elusive elements produced sounds that were often harder to describe and impossible to evaluate whether they are "accurate" or not.
